Hearing project · first-pass models · seed 1

H vs C listening test

Four test sentences, each with a loud everyday transient on top. Press play, then switch between versions while it plays: the switch keeps your place, so you hear the same moment through each model.

Turn your volume down before you press play. The transient's peak is 15 dB louder than the speech, so the unprocessed version is loud. Headphones make the differences easier to hear.

C · Keshavarzi et al. 2021 RNN, the original transient-reduction model
H · proposed: same filterbank + belief network + attention

How these four were chosen

  • By rule, from the 1,800 scored test mixtures, before listening. All at STR −15 dB, the hardest level used in training.
  • Typical = the sentence whose extra transient removal (H minus C) is the median for that kind of sound. Best and worst = the largest and smallest H-minus-C gain.
  • Two of the four are crinkling only because H's worst case happened to be a crinkle. The best and worst cases are the tails, not the norm: over all 600 crinkling test mixtures on each of 3 training seeds, H and C remove the same amount on average (H removes more on 46% of them). H's gains are on impact sounds it never heard.
  • At STR −15 dB the crinkle's loudest part is still about as loud as the speech after either model, so it stays clearly audible. At gentler levels both leave less behind.
  • Every talker, sentence and transient here was held out of training. Crinkling sounds and the impact-sound categories were never heard at all.
  • Models from training seed 1, the median seed for both C and H (seed 0 was C's weakest, which would flatter H).
  • Transient removed: how much quieter the transient got (dB). Speech preserved: STOI of the speech alone after the same processing (1.000 = untouched). STOI: predicted intelligibility of the output.
  • Averages over all test sentences and three training seeds are in results/stage4/README.md.
